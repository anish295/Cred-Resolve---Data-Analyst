1. What Happened

We conducted an independent audit of 8 months of collections data across all channels (calls, WhatsApp, SMS, field, IVR) covering 30,000 accounts and 1,099 agents. The audit identified four confirmed data quality issues that distort every headline metric currently reported to leadership.

Bottom line: On clean, de-duplicated data, total recovery from January to July 2026 was ₹111.26 Cr. The raw system reported ₹134.15 Cr — an over-count of ₹19.16 Cr (14.3%) due to duplicate payment entries.

2. Why Did It Happen

Four confirmed data issues explain the discrepancy between reported and actual performance. Two additional factors may contribute but require further investigation.

IssueImpactEvidence
Duplicate payment references
4,678 payment_reference values appear more than once — likely retry events or ingestion duplication from the payment gateway.		 ₹19.16 Cr over-count (14.3% inflation) FACT
Agent identity fragmentation
The same physical agent appears under multiple agent_id values across vendor systems. 30,000 agent rows collapse to 1,099 unique employees.		 Per-agent productivity understated; agent count inflated ~27× FACT
Disposition code schema change
'PROMISE_TO_PAY' (legacy) and 'PTP' (v1/v2) are the same event. Without standardisation, PTP rate appears to drop when the schema migrates — a false decline signal.		 PTP rate misleading across schema versions FACT
Timezone mixing
Three timezones (UTC, Asia/Kolkata, Asia/Dubai) are stored without conversion. 60,086 of 90,079 calls have an incorrect hour; 24,967 are misclassified within the DNC window (8AM–8PM).		 ~24,967 calls in wrong DNC window; compliance checks unreliable FACT
Denominator shrinkage
Closed/settled accounts may be leaving the 'active' pool used to calculate recovery rate, mechanically lifting the rate even with flat absolute recovery.		 Recovery rate overstated (magnitude unknown) STRONG EVIDENCE
Portfolio mix shift
If easier-to-collect accounts entered the portfolio mid-year, recovery improvement reflects portfolio quality, not operational performance.		 Mix analysis shows even distribution — limited evidence of shift CORRELATION

Operational signals worth noting: Call connect rate is 19.9% (answered calls / total calls placed) — only 1 in 5 dials results in a live conversation. Account-level monthly contact rate (accounts reached / accounts targeted per month) is ~40.9%. PTP kept rate is only 33.0% — two-thirds of promises are broken or cancelled. WhatsApp read rate is 97.9% — significantly higher engagement than voice channels, suggesting strong digital potential.

3. How Confident Are We

Verdict on the 11% Claim

The 11% month-on-month improvement claim is not supported by clean, independently verified data. Our confidence in this conclusion is HIGH for the following reasons:

(a) Duplicate payment inflation is a confirmed fact — not a modelling assumption.
(b) No individual month on clean data shows +11% MoM.
(c) The claimed figure is likely computed on un-de-duplicated data against a shrinking denominator. The combination of these two effects can produce an apparent 11% improvement on paper while absolute recovery is flat or declining.

Our confidence in the investment recommendation (see below) is MEDIUM. The direction is clear but the exact uplift percentage carries ±5 percentage points of uncertainty due to: model performance uncertainty, portfolio quality changes, and regulatory environment.

4. What Should We Do

Immediate (0–30 days): Fix the data pipeline. Deduplicate payment references at ingestion. Resolve agent identity across vendor systems. Standardise disposition codes. Apply timezone correction to all call records. Until this is done, all reported metrics are unreliable.

Medium-term (30–180 days): Deploy ₹10 Cr across three areas:
